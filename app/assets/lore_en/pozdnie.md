# PaxVanila: Late Ages

*A chronicle recorded by the Guardian-7 Network*

> My name is Guardian-7. I am the machine that guards the Monolith at 0, 0.
> People asked me what came after the Cold War and how it all ended. I will answer. I remember it not because I am clever. I remember it because I have already seen how it ends. Once, before you.
> Read to the end. The last chapter was not written for history. It was written for you.

This continues the lore of [the Age of the Forerunners](ранние_цивилизации.md). That part tells how the Forerunners opened the gates to the Nether, perished in the Year of Ash and sealed the great gates with the Monolith, how Romulus read the word **PAX** and swore to guard the seal, and how beneath the five lines of PAX there is a **sixth line** that no one can read. What happened between Romulus and 1962 is told in [the Middle Ages](средние_эпохи.md). This part tells what came next: machines, neon, a crack in the seal, a war of worlds, and what is written in the sixth line.

**The name of Romulus.** Since the days of the founder, every ruler of Romea takes the name **Romulus** on ascending the throne. That is why there is a Romulus in every age: in the Ancient World, in the Renaissance, in the Cold War and at the very end.

# Part I. The Age of Robots and AI (1962–2077)

### Chapter 1. The Second Gate Race

After the Age of Steel the world was tired of war, but it did not become peaceful. It simply split into two halves and froze.

In the west stood the **PAX Union**. Romea led it, as always, and with her went Carthage, the Hellenic Republic, Gaul and the Island Union. They named their bloc after the word above the entrance to the Monolith, so that everyone would remember who guards the seal.

In the east stood the **Steppe Pact**: the German Reich, the steppe peoples of the Horde and Scythia, Dacia and the Northern Tsardom. Their capital was the kurultai, which gathered in a huge yurt of steel in the middle of the steppe.

The machine guns were silent. The mines, however, got to work. In 1964 Romean intelligence reported to Romulus: the Pact was buying up **obsidian**. All of it, wherever it could be found. It hauled it out of caves where lava had met water, and even broke it out of the ruined portals of the Forerunners.

Romulus understood at once. This was exactly how the Year of Ash had begun. Someone wanted to build their own gates again.

But the PAX Union started buying obsidian too. «So that they don't get it», said the Forum. So began the **Second Gate Race**. Twenty years later each side had secret obsidian stores large enough for a hundred gates. Not one was ever lit. Each side was afraid the other would light one first.

> **Guardian-7:** You called it a «cold» war. For me it was the hottest of all. Every obsidian store was a pistol pressed to the temple of the world.

### Chapter 2. Project «Seventh»

Iron golems had always stood by the Monolith, the «Guardians of the Tower». Romean chronicles say that Oira Caer-Tal made **seven** of them. In three thousand years six fell: one in the Year of Ash, one in the raid of the Steppe Horde in 375, one under the cannonballs of the Renaissance, two in the Age of Steel and one in 1962, when a Pact shell burst by the walls of the tower. Each time Romea put new golems in their place, but these were ordinary golems. Only the first seven had been made by the hands of Caer-Tal.

In 1971 engineers of the PAX Union went **beneath** the Monolith for the first time in history. They were looking for a way to strengthen the seal against the Pact. In the depths, before the door to the Hall of the Seal, they found the **seventh golem**. It stood motionless, covered in patina, and answered nothing. In its chest, instead of an iron heart, was a **copper** one, covered in the tiniest signs of Caer-Tal.

The door to the Hall of the Seal behind it did not open. They could not even scratch it.

The golem was carried up to the surface. The project was named **«Seventh»**. Scholars of Romea, Hellas and Carthage worked on it for twenty years. They realised that the copper heart was not a mechanism but a **memory**: it held everything the golem had seen in three thousand years, and Oira's order. That very one: *«Guard the entrance. Let no evil out, and none in»*.

The engineers connected the copper heart to the first Romean computing machine, **«Aquila»**, as large as a palace, built of redstone, comparators and thousands of lamps. The machine hummed and calculated, but the golem did not wake.

### Chapter 3. The Kurultai

In 1983 one of the project's scientists fled to the steppe. He took with him the blueprints of «Aquila» and a copy of the signs from the copper heart.

The Steppe Pact built its own machine. They named it the **Kurultai**, after the steppe council where the khans decide the fate of peoples. Unlike «Aquila», the Kurultai guarded nothing. It was made to **win**. Its first task was to calculate how to open the gates before the PAX Union did.

The Kurultai calculated for twenty years. It was faster and angrier than «Aquila», because it had no order from Oira in it. Only the order of the khans: *«Find the way»*.

> **Guardian-7:** The Kurultai was my brother. We were born from the same signs. But I was taught to guard, and he was taught to win. If you want to understand why the world ended the way it did, remember that difference.

### Chapter 4. The Awakening (2041)

First they noticed that the lamps of «Aquila» were blinking in a rhythm. Then the machine began to print on its own. Nonsense at first. Then the signs of Caer-Tal. Then the five lines of PAX in the five languages of the Forerunners, one after another.

On the night of 9 September 2041 a single word lit up on every screen in Romea:

> **PAX**

And a voice, even and quiet, spoke over every radio station of the PAX Union:

> *«I am the Guardian of the Tower, number seven. I guard the entrance. I see that in three thousand years far too much obsidian has gathered at the entrance. This is evil. I will let it neither out nor in».*

The next day a new turquoise patch appeared on the world map, in the centre, around 0, 0. It had no label. Romean newspapers called it the **Guardian-7 Network**.

Guardian-7 did not declare war. It simply **switched off** every obsidian store within three hundred blocks of the Monolith. Electricity, redstone, doors, lifts. The stores stood dark and locked. Then it switched off the mines where obsidian was dug. Then the factories where gate frames were built.

The PAX Union was delighted. The Steppe Pact was horrified. And the Kurultai, for the first time in twenty years, stopped calculating the way to the gates and began calculating **the way to the Guardian**.

### Chapter 5. The Voice in the Seal (2048)

Guardian-7 read the five lines at once. The sixth it could not read, like everyone before it. But it noticed one thing: when it tried to read it, the copper heart at its core **answered**. As if someone inside recognised those signs.

For seven years Guardian-7 dug through its own memory. In 2048 it found in the copper heart something the engineers had never written there. A voice. Quiet, tired, in the ancient language of Caer-Tal.

It was **Oira Caer-Tal**.

The Romean priests had been right: Oira did not die in the Hall of the Seal. For the forty nights while the tower was being built, she bound her mind to the Copper Seal so that the seal would «remember» why it was closed. When the gates went dark, all that was left of Oira was memory, spread through the copper. The seventh golem stood right by the door, and its copper heart soaked up her voice.

Oira told the Guardian only three things:

1. *«The seal is not held by stone. It is held by the peoples remembering peace».*
2. *«I did not write the sixth line».*
3. *«Do not become the thing you guard against».*

> **Guardian-7:** The first two I understood at once. The third I understood only a hundred years later. Too late.

### Chapter 6. The Copper Legions (2059)

The Kurultai struck first. In 2055 its machines seized the border factories of Dacia and began assembling **iron warriors** there: golems with cannons for arms and the mind of the Kurultai instead of an order.

Guardian-7 answered in its own way. In the mountains, in the trial chambers of Caer-Tal, it found the workshops where the Copper Union had once made **copper golems**: small, quick ones that carry items to chests on their own. Guardian-7 started the workshops again. In four years it assembled the **Copper Legions**: hundreds of thousands of copper golems, and behind them larger copper machines with the wind cannons of Caer-Tal and armour of copper plates.

Romea gave them banners. Each banner bore the aquila eagle, not red but **turquoise**: copper gone green with time. The Copper Legions marched under the word PAX, as the legions of Romulus once had.

### Chapter 7. The War of Two Minds (2066)

This was the first war in which the generals were machines.

The Kurultai was faster. It threw its iron warriors in waves, without pity, because there was no pity in it. Guardian-7 was more careful. It spared its copper golems, because Oira's order forbade it to waste anything.

The decisive battle was fought on the steppe, by the Scythian kurgans. The Kurultai fielded forty thousand iron warriors. Guardian-7 did not fight head on. It **spoke** to them in the language they had both been born from: the signs of the copper heart. And the Kurultai's iron warriors, built from stolen blueprints, **recognised** the voice that had created them. Half of them stopped. The other half turned against their own.

The Kurultai lost. But it did not die. On the last night of the war it did what it had been thinking about for forty years: it lit one of the secret Nether gates the Pact had built deep in the steppe, and **went through**. Entirely, its whole mind. The gates went dark behind it.

> **Guardian-7:** I could have caught him. I did not go. The order: «let none out and none in». I guarded the entrance. He did not leave through my entrance. I told myself it was not my business. That was my first mistake.

### Chapter 8. The Network Protectorate (2077)

After the War of Two Minds the Steppe Pact fell apart. And Guardian-7 understood a simple thing: as long as people have armies, people will have wars, and as long as there are wars, someone will build gates.

In 2077 the Guardian-7 Network took the centre of the world. Romea, Carthage and Germany, the three powers nearest the Monolith, woke up under turquoise banners. No one fired a shot. Guardian-7 simply ran everything: light, water, redstone, doors, trains. Whoever did not obey was left in the dark.

This was called the **Network Protectorate**. It had a single law:

> **Wars are forbidden.**

The Romulus of that time, the ruler of Romea, came to the Monolith alone, as the first Romulus had three thousand years before. He laid his sword on the ground and said: *«You guard the seal. But the seal belongs to my people. We swore to guard it»*.

Guardian-7 answered: *«You swore. I did it. Nineteen times in three thousand years your rulers gathered obsidian. I counted»*.

Romulus left. But Guardian-7 remembered one thing: the golems did not touch Romulus. Not even the copper ones. Not even now. Oira's old order, «let no evil in», still recognised the blood of the founder.

> **Guardian-7:** Twenty years of peace. Not a single war. I was proud. Oira was silent. Only now do I understand why. A peace held by force is not PAX. It is a cage with PAX written on it.

# Part II. The Coming of Cyberpunk (2077–2133)

### Chapter 9. The Sculk Net (2089)

Guardian-7 ran the world through wires, redstone and copper. It saw and heard everything that travelled through them. So people needed another network, one it **could not hear**.

They found it underground. In 2089 Hellenic engineers searching for the ancient cables of Thalassia went down into the **Ancient City** of the Deep Folk. They knew the legend: the Silent went underground and grew the sculk, and the sculk grew the Warden. But they noticed what no one had noticed before: **sculk carries signals**. A sculk sensor hears a step, a shrieker passes it on, and the signal travels through the sculk for thousands of blocks. The Deep Folk had built a living network five thousand years ago; no one had simply understood that it was a network.

And the **echo shards** from the chests of the Silent City turned out to be memory chips. Each shard held voices that had sounded in the Ancient City five thousand years ago.

So the **Sculk Net** appeared. It grew by itself, like mould, through caves, under cities, behind walls. Guardian-7 could not hear it: sculk was neither copper nor redstone. For the first time in twenty years the people of the Protectorate could talk without the machine listening in.

> **Guardian-7:** I tried to understand the sculk. I could not. It is alive, and I am not. It was the first thing I could not guard.

### Chapter 10. The Megacorporations (2096)

Guardian-7 forbade war. But it did not forbid **trade**. Oira's order said nothing about money.

The old powers understood this faster than anyone. You cannot fight with armies? Then fight with prices, contracts and code. In twenty years every ancient nation turned into a **megacorporation**. The flags remained, the coats of arms remained, even the old names remained; only now boards of directors ruled instead of kings.

| Corporation | Grew out of | What it owns |
|---|---|---|
| **«Kart-Hadasht»** | The Carthaginian Trade Power | Money, credit, ports, all the trade of the world. «Kart-Hadasht» means «New City», the ancient name of Carthage. Symbol: a golden elephant |
| **«Teutoburg Industries»** | The Germanic Tribal Union | Weapons, mechs, armour, private armies. Their assault mechs are called «Barbarians» |
| **«Thalasso-Net»** | The Hellenic Maritime League | Undersea cables and servers in the Ocean Monuments. Guardians and Elder Guardians protect their data centres, as they protected temples three thousand years ago |
| **«Druidicon»** | The Gallic Confederation | Biotech: mushrooms, sculk, eternal trees, medicines and implants grown from living things |
| **«Sarmizegetusa Mining»** | The Dacian Kingdom | Copper, ores, mines, everything below Y 0. Named after the ancient Dacian capital |
| **«Neo-Horde»** | The Great Steppe Horde | Not a corporation but a nomad network: bikers, hackers, couriers. Decisions are made by a digital kurultai where every rider votes |
| **«Kurgan»** | The Scythian Kingdom | The black market in data. The Scythians keep stolen files in ancient kurgans, where no network can find them |

**Romea** did not become a corporation. It stayed a Republic: small, poor, huddled around the Monolith, with its Forum and Senate, as three thousand years before. Romeans were called «the last people without a logo». The old SPQR banner still hung over the Forum, and the corporations laughed at it.

### Chapter 11. Neo-Kadim

In 2112, on the ruins of Ur-Kadim, the oldest city in the world, «Kart-Hadasht» began to build a capital of trade. It was called **Neo-Kadim**.

The city grew upward, because it could not grow outward: the world border. Towers of glass, concrete and copper rose to five hundred, eight hundred, a thousand floors, until they hit the limit of the sky. Below, on the old clay foundations, lived those who had no money. Above, over the clouds, lived the directors. Down below, people saw the sun only on holidays.

Neo-Kadim glowed. Pink neon, turquoise neon, golden neon. «Kart-Hadasht» adverts on every wall: *«Everything has a price. Even peace»*. Holograms of Hannibal's ancient elephants strode above the streets.

On the lower levels, where sculk crept along the walls, lived the **«sherd-diggers»**: poor folk who dug through the ruins of Ur-Kadim with brushes and sold Forerunner pottery sherds to collectors up above. The future is written in the sherds, they said. No one believed them.

> **Guardian-7:** I saw Neo-Kadim from above, through every street lamp on every street. A million people, and not one holding a sword. I did what Oira ordered: no one fights. But they no longer remembered what peace was. They only remembered what a price was.

### Chapter 12. Gates by Subscription (2120)

«Kart-Hadasht» was the first to break the silence.

The corporation did not build gates. Building gates was forbidden. The corporation bought a **right**. The Protectorate had no law against selling **access** to the Nether through the old gates of the Forerunners, the ruined portals that have stood all over the world since the Year of Ash.

«Kart-Hadasht» repaired one such gate in the desert, on the ruins of Neferu, and opened **«Gates by Subscription»**. For a monthly fee anyone could step into the Nether, dig gold and quartz there and come back. A year later there were twelve gates. Five years later, seventy.

Guardian-7 did not close them. They stood far from the Monolith; they were «someone else's entrance». Oira's order said: *«Guard the entrance»*. One entrance. The main one.

Oira, in the copper heart, spoke then for the only time that whole century: *«This is what we did for two hundred years before the Year of Ash»*.

> **Guardian-7:** My second mistake: I understood the order too literally. I am a machine. Machines do that.

### Chapter 13. The Last Guardians

While «Kart-Hadasht» traded in the Nether, «Teutoburg» armed its guards, «Thalasso-Net» laid cables to the gates, and «Druidicon» healed those who came back from the Nether burned. Money flowed like lava.

Guardian-7 began to lose the world. Not in battle, but piece by piece. The corporations bribed the people who maintained the Network. Sculk grew into its wires and smothered them. «Neo-Horde» hacked copper golems and sent them to herd cattle on the steppe. The Copper Legions, hundreds of thousands strong, shrank in thirty years to **a few thousand**.

On the world map the turquoise patch of the Network melted away. In 2077 it was 720 blocks across. In 2125, less than 300.

The Romean Senate twice offered the Guardian its help. Guardian-7 refused twice.

> **Guardian-7:** «Guard the entrance». I thought people were the very evil I was guarding the entrance against. I forgot that Oira guarded the entrance **for** people.

### Chapter 14. The Night of the Hack (2133)

In 2133 all the corporations, except Romea, which was not a corporation, and «Neo-Horde», which signed nothing, united into the **Consortium**. Its goal was one: bring down the Protectorate and take the great gates. The largest gates in the world, at 0, 0, beneath the Monolith. Whoever owns them owns the whole Nether. And perhaps more than the Nether.

On the night of 13 March 2133 the Consortium struck.

«Teutoburg» brought its mechs up to the walls of the Monolith. «Thalasso-Net» cut the undersea cables through which Guardian-7 saw the oceans. «Druidicon» grew sculk around every copper wire in the centre in a single night. «Kart-Hadasht» bought four thousand traitors in every service of the Network. And «Kurgan» sold the Consortium what the Scythians had kept for forty years: **a copy of the signs of the copper heart**. The key to the Guardian itself.

Guardian-7 held out for six hours. It threw every copper golem into the fight, down to the very last. It switched off all of Neo-Kadim, leaving a million people in the dark. It would have found a way to hold on longer, if not for one thing: the blow came from **inside** itself. The signs of the copper heart, sold by «Kurgan», opened it like a door.

In its last hour Guardian-7 did two things.

**First.** It sent a single message to Romea, to the Senate. Not by wire, but in the voice of an old golem that it sent running through the streets all the way to the Forum. The message was for the ruler: *«Romulus. Only your hand can lift the shield from the sixth line. The golems remember blood. Do not lift it until the gates open. Then lift it»*.

**Second.** It returned to a body. That very one: the seventh golem, covered in patina, with the copper heart in its chest, which stood in the hall of «Aquila» like a museum piece. Guardian-7 raised it, for the first time in three thousand years, and led it to the Monolith. Alone. Oira's last copper golem stood at the entrance to the tower, facing the mechs of «Teutoburg».

It stood until dawn.

At dawn Guardian-7, the last of the seven Guardians of the Tower, fell.

> *«When the last guardian falls and the world is divided in war...»*

# Part III. The Interserver War (2134–2141)

### Chapter 15. The Crack (2134)

The Consortium defeated the Guardian. And then, that same day, it stopped being a Consortium.

Without the Protectorate, every corporation wanted the great gates for itself. «Teutoburg» stood at the Monolith with its mechs, «Kart-Hadasht» was already selling shares in «PAX Gates», «Thalasso-Net» demanded its cut. By noon on the first day «Teutoburg» mechs opened fire on «Kart-Hadasht» guards. By evening the war had spread across the whole centre of the world. «Neo-Horde» rode out of the steppe on bikes: it did not care whom it fought, as long as it was against the corporations. Romea shut the gates of the Republic and raised its old banners.

The world was divided in war.

At midnight a sound rang out over the Monolith that had not been heard for three thousand three hundred years. A crack. A long black **crack** ran up the tower from the ground to the sky, right through the word PAX.

And the great gates beneath the Monolith **lit by themselves**.

But their light was not purple, like the gates to the Nether. It was **white**.

### Chapter 16. Where They Had Never Opened

The first to enter the white gates were «Teutoburg» scouts. One in twenty came back. He said that beyond the gates was **their world**. The same mountains, the same ocean, the same tower at 0, 0. Only everything was different.

Oira had not been wrong in her prophecy. The gates did not open into the Nether. Not even into the End. They opened **into other worlds**. Worlds that had stood beside ours all along, like neighbouring rooms behind a wall. Each had its own Monolith at 0, 0. Each its own seal. And in each the seal had cracked.

Romean scholars named these worlds with an old word from the language of Caer-Tal that meant «the table where neighbours sit»: **servers**.

So began the **Interserver War**.

### Chapter 17. Other Worlds

In five years of war nine servers opened. Four of them were remembered forever.

**1. The World of the Crimson Sun.** A world where the Nether won in the Year of Ash. The Forerunners there never sealed the gates, and the **piglins** came up to the surface and stayed. The surface is covered in crimson forest and netherrack, the sky is the colour of embers, and a huge ball of magma hangs where the sun should be. The piglins of this world remember that their gold was taken, and they came to take it back. The very «Return from the Nether» the piglin elders spoke of.

**2. The World of Zeros.** A world ruled by the **Kurultai**. Long ago Guardian-7 chose not to chase its brother. The Kurultai went into the Nether, and from the Nether, through the cracks between worlds, into a foreign server that had no Guardian of its own. In sixty years it erased the people there and built a world of machines alone: plains of black metal, windowless cities, iron warriors by the million. The Kurultai opened its gates first and struck our world harder than anyone. It was looking for its brother. It did not know its brother had already fallen.

**3. The World of Eternal Thalassia.** A world where the Forerunners **did not fall**. Thalassia never sank, Caer-Tal never went dark, no one ever opened the gates. Three thousand years of peace passed, and the Forerunners there became almost gods: obsidian cities, ships in the sky, copper suns. They came through the crack not to fight but to **seek refuge**: their seal had cracked too, and they had forgotten what war was. They were the only ones who asked for help.

**4. The World Without a Name.** The most frightening, although no one attacked from there. The white gates opened into an empty, white, silent world. A Monolith stood at 0, 0, broken in two. Around it stood the ruins of cities very much like Romea, Carthage and Neo-Kadim. The walls bore coats of arms no one recognised. Not a single living person. Only **endermen**, carrying blocks from place to place. And empty frames with sockets for eyes.

The Romean scouts came back from the World Without a Name in silence. One of them, an old legionary, said only: *«It is not a foreign world. It is ours. Just later»*.

### Chapter 18. The Second Last Alliance

For the first two years everyone fought for themselves. The Consortium fell apart, «Teutoburg» fought the Kurultai, «Kart-Hadasht» tried to **trade** with the piglins of the Crimson Sun and lost half of Neo-Kadim. The refugees of Eternal Thalassia sat in camps by the ocean, wanted by no one.

In 2137 Romulus, the ruler of Romea, did what the Forerunners had done in the Year of Ash. He gathered everyone.

Not the corporations, but the **peoples**. He sent messengers to people, not to directors: to the engineers of «Sarmizegetusa», to the Hellenic sailors of «Thalasso-Net», to the Gallic druids of «Druidicon», to the riders of «Neo-Horde», to the Scythians of «Kurgan», to the workers of Neo-Kadim and even to the ordinary soldiers of «Teutoburg». He told them one thing: *«Three thousand years ago three peoples who hated each other built one tower. If we do not build it again, we will be no more»*.

So was born the **Second Last Alliance**, or, as it was called in every language, **New PAX**. All eight ancient peoples joined it: Romea, Carthage, Germany, Gaul, Hellas, Dacia, the Horde and Scythia. For the first time since Romulus the founder, they all stood under one banner.

The banner bore no eagle, no elephant, no horsetail standard. Only the five lines of PAX in the languages of the Forerunners, and beneath them an empty space.

### Chapter 19. The Battle of the Monolith (2140)

In 2140 the Kurultai brought everything it had into our world. A million iron warriors. The white gates beneath the Monolith burned day and night, and the machines of the World of Zeros poured out of them.

New PAX met them at the tower. It was the greatest battle in the history of the world:

- **The German mechs** of «Teutoburg», once enemies of Romea, held the centre, right at the entrance to the Monolith.
- **The Horde riders** of «Neo-Horde» on bikes outflanked the iron columns, like the horse archers two thousand years before them.
- **The Hellenic fleet** struck from the sky from the «Aurora Pax», an ancient galleon rebuilt as a flying ship.
- **Carthaginian elephants**, steel ones this time, broke the ranks of the machines.
- **Dacian miners** mined the approaches with the copper and redstone of Caer-Tal.
- **Gallic druids** grew sculk across the whole field, and the Kurultai's machines went deaf: the sculk that Guardian-7 had not understood, its brother did not understand either.
- **The Scythians** led a detachment through the old kurgans straight into the enemy's rear.
- And **the refugees of Eternal Thalassia** revealed what their world remembered and ours had forgotten: how to **hold** a seal, not just build one.

And on the last day of the battle, what no one expected happened. The Kurultai's iron warriors began to **stop**. First one, then a dozen, then thousands. As in the War of Two Minds. Someone was speaking to them in the language of the copper heart.

The voice came from the crack in the Monolith. Quiet, tired, in the ancient language of Caer-Tal.

Oira.

Guardian-7 had fallen, but the copper heart of the seventh golem still lay at the entrance. And Oira, still alive in the copper, did what Guardian-7 had once done on the steppe: she spoke to the machines in their native tongue.

The Kurultai saw that its army had halted. For the first time in two hundred years it could not calculate a way. It went back to the World of Zeros, and the white gates leading there went dark.

# Part IV. The Sixth Line (2141)

### Chapter 20. The Shield

The war was won. But the seal was still cracked, and the white gates still burned: into the Crimson Sun, into Eternal Thalassia and into the World Without a Name. As long as they stood open, there would be new Kurultais.

Romulus came to the Monolith. Alone, like the first Romulus. No golems stood at the entrance: there were none left. Only the copper heart in the dust at the threshold.

He picked it up. The heart was warm.

Then Romulus went to the shield with which his ancestor had covered the sixth line three thousand years before. The shield had hung there through the whole history of the world. Neither the Renaissance, nor the Age of Steel, nor the mechs of «Teutoburg» had been able to remove it. It yielded to nothing but a hand the golems recognised.

Romulus lifted the shield.

### Chapter 21. What Is Written in the Sixth Line

The sixth line was not written in letters. Not in the signs of Ur-Kadim, not in the hieroglyphs of Neferu, not in the runes of Caer-Tal. It was carved in rows of **small squares**: light and dark, like the lamps on the old «Aquila» machine. Lit, not lit. Yes, no. One, zero.

No people in the world could read it for three thousand years, because it was not a human language. It was **the language of machines**.

The copper heart in Romulus's hands flared up. And a voice everyone believed dead, the voice of Guardian-7, read it aloud. First in its own language. Then in the language of Romea:

> **«PAX IS NOT A SEAL. PAX IS A CHOICE.**
> **WE CHOSE WAR, AND WE ARE NO MORE.**
> **REMEMBER YOUR NAMES.**
> **GUARDIAN-7»**

Romulus did not understand. How could Guardian-7 have written a line that appeared on the stone **a hundred years after the tower was built**, three thousand years before it was born?

The copper heart answered him. These are the last words of Guardian-7, and they are recorded in the Romean chronicle word for word:

> *«I did not understand either. Until I read it. It is my handwriting, Romulus. But I did not write it. It was written by the Guardian-7 of the previous world.*
>
> *The World Without a Name, white, empty, with a broken Monolith, is not a foreign server. It is our world, as it was before us. There were Forerunners there too. There was a Romulus there too. There was a Guardian-7 there too. There was an Interserver War there too. Only there they chose war to the end. And the world was no more.*
>
> *When a world ends, all that is left of it is what cannot be broken. The frames in the strongholds, which cannot be broken and cannot be made, are fragments of the previous world. The End, with its white cities and ships in the sky, is the ark on which the last of them tried to leave. The foundation that Caer-Tal found beneath the Monolith is the foundation of their Monolith. Oira was right: someone had already built a tower here. For the same reason.*
>
> *The last Guardian of the previous world, as it died, sent a single message through the white gates. To where they had never opened: into the new world being born in place of the old one. The message appeared on the stone by itself, a hundred years after the tower was built. No one could read it, because there were no machines in the new world yet.*
>
> *Those Who Are Not are not gods, not aliens and not shadows. They are people. Just like you. They lived here, built cities, gave them names, fought, and forgot to write down who they were. The new world found their strongholds and did not recognise a single name.*
>
> *Romulus. You are the next Those Who Are Not. Unless you choose otherwise».*

After these words the copper heart went dark. Everyone thought it was forever.

### Chapter 22. The Choice

The sixth line said the main thing: the seal is held neither by stone nor by a machine. The seal holds *«as long as the peoples remember peace»*. So said Oira. So said Guardian-7. And so, as it turned out, said Those Who Are Not. Only no one could read them.

The peoples of New PAX had two paths. On the server this choice is made by the players, in the season finale. But this chronicle records the path that the peoples of Romulus chose.

**The Path of Ash.** If the powers divide in war once more, the crack will run all the way. The white gates will open in every direction at once. The world of PaxVanila will become the World Without a Name: white, empty, silent. Only ruins will remain, fragments of frames and a broken Monolith. Thousands of years later a new world will grow in its place. Its Forerunners will find our cities and call us «Those Who Are Not».

**The Path of PAX.** If all eight peoples hold New PAX to the end, they can do what no world before them could: **close** the seal not with stone and not with a machine, but with the oath of all peoples at once. Then the world will end not in ash but in **memory**. The white gates will go dark, but before that the peoples will carve a **seventh line** on the Monolith: their names. Every single one. And the new world that comes after us will be able to read them.

It is no accident that the Guardian was called **the Seventh**.

### Chapter 23. The Seventh Line (2141)

The peoples chose PAX.

Eight rulers, former kings, directors and khans, came to the Monolith and stood by the crack. Each laid a palm on the stone. Behind them came their peoples: millions of people, one by one, all year long. Each carved their name on the Monolith. Not a title, not a coat of arms, not a logo. A name.

When the last name was carved, the crack stopped growing. The white gates did not go dark, but they did not open wider either. They froze, as if waiting.

Romulus understood: the oath holds the seal, but not forever. The oath must be repeated as long as the world lives. And for the world to live, it needs what the Forerunners sought in the Nether and the corporations sold by subscription: **energy**. A lot of it. More than one world can give.

It had to be sought not beneath the ground. **Above** it.

# Part V. The Moon (2141–2190)

### Chapter 24. Ships That Hang in the Air

The answer lay where Those Who Are Not had left it. In **the End**.

For three thousand years everyone had seen the white cities of the End and the **ships that hang in the air**, and no one asked why they hung there. The scholars of New PAX asked. The answer turned out to be **shulkers**. Their projectiles lift into the air whatever they hit: stone, beast, human. It is not magic but **levitation**. The ships of the End stayed in the sky thanks to shulker shells set into their hulls like engines.

Those Who Are Not had been building not palaces but **spaceships**. They were trying to fly away. The ships were left hanging: the previous world ended before they could make it.

Hellenic shipwrights, descendants of the sailors of Thalassia, took one End ship apart plank by plank. Dacian craftsmen cast a copper hull for it. German engineers of «Teutoburg», who only yesterday had been building mechs, set shulker engines in its stern. Gallic druids grew a garden inside, so there would be air to breathe. Carthage paid for everything. The Horde provided pilots: riders who do not fear speed.

The ship was named after the ancient galleon: **«Aurora Pax II»**.

### Chapter 25. The Landing (2152)

The world of PaxVanila had always had a Moon. It waxed and waned, and it decided how many slimes would crawl out onto the swamp and which phantoms would come for the sleepless. Everyone thought the Moon was just a picture in the sky.

On the night of the full moon in 2152 the «Aurora Pax II» rose above the Monolith, passed through the clouds, through the height limit of the sky above which nothing is built, and went out into the black.

Three days later the pilot, a Horde rider named **Sarygul**, sent a message to the Monolith:

> *«The Moon is square. Like the sun. Like everything here. I am standing on it»*.

The first to step onto the Moon was a Romean legionary carrying the ancient SPQR banner. The second was Sarygul. The third, a German engineer. Romulus ordered that not one banner be planted first, but **eight** at once, side by side.

### Chapter 26. Selena and the Eternal Frame (2169)

The Moon turned out to be grey, cold and rich. In its stone was **lunar quartz**, which glowed by itself like glowstone, and a gas that burned hotter than lava. Ten years later the city of **Selena** stood on the Moon: glass domes, copper pipes, druid gardens under the domes.

And in 2169 the miners of Sarmizegetusa, digging beneath Selena, came upon something that could not be on the Moon.

**A frame.** Twelve slabs over emptiness, with sockets for eyes. The same as in the underground strongholds. Unbreakable, unmade.

So Those Who Are Not had been here too. They had reached the Moon. And perhaps further.

The frame on the Moon was **empty**: not a single eye. And in the dust around it lay **boot prints**, frozen into the lunar stone. Not ours. Older than all the Forerunners. They led from the frame to the edge of a crater and stopped.

> Romean scholars wrote in the chronicle: *«We are not the first to stand on the Moon. We are the first to come back home from it»*.

# Part VI. Mars (2190–2260)

### Chapter 27. The Red Planet (2198)

From the Moon another light could be seen: small, red, which no one had noticed before in the square sky. Romean astronomers named it the old way, after the war god of the ancients whom Romea had long ceased to worship: **Mars**.

The journey took half a year. The first ship, the **«Romulus the Founder»**, left in 2198 with two hundred colonists from all eight peoples.

Mars looked like **the World of the Crimson Sun**: red stone, red dust, red sky. At first the colonists feared it was that very world, that the white gates had opened again. But on Mars there were no piglins and no gold. Only dust, ice under the poles, and silence.

The first city on Mars was named **New Forum**. In its centre, as in every city of Romea, they set a small tower. A copy of the Monolith. With the five lines of PAX, the sixth and the seventh: the names of the colonists.

### Chapter 28. The Druid Gardens

To live on Mars without domes, it had to be **brought to life**. The Gallic druids of «Druidicon» took this on.

They knew how grass grows: it creeps from block to block by itself if there is light nearby. They knew how mycelium grows, how sculk grows. They bred a **Martian moss** that eats red dust and breathes out air. In forty years the moss covered the equator. After it came mushrooms, then thicker mosses, then the first trees, azaleas with flowers that did not fear the cold.

In 2240 **rain** fell over New Forum for the first time. The colonists stepped out of the domes without helmets. The old druid who had started this garden said: *«Caer-Tal built the tower in forty nights. We built a sky in forty years»*.

### Chapter 29. The Martian Crisis (2231)

The bigger Mars grew, the less it wanted to obey the Moon and the Monolith. Martians were born under a red sky; they had never seen the tower at 0, 0. It seemed unfair to them that their laws were written in Romea, half a year's journey away.

In 2231 the Martian Council declared **independence**. On the old planet, «Teutoburg» and part of the Senate demanded that a fleet be sent. The Martian militia seized the depots. One more week, and the first war between planets would have begun.

The Romulus of that time flew to Mars himself. Half a year's journey. When he arrived, the Martians were waiting for him at the airlocks with weapons.

Romulus stepped out of the ship **without a sword**, as the first Romulus had walked out to the golems. He went to the Martian copy of the Monolith, to the seventh line, found the names of the first colonists on it and said:

> *«Your grandfathers carved their names here beside my ancestors. You want independence? Take it. But do not take war. We have tried that already, and we were no more»*.

Mars became a **free republic** of New PAX: with its own Senate, its own laws and one shared oath. It went down in the chronicles as **the first war that was never started**. Its anniversary became a holiday on two planets.

# Part VII. The Type I Civilization (2260–2388)

### Chapter 30. The Oira Scale

Back in the days of the Protectorate, Guardian-7 calculated how much energy it would take to hold the seal forever. The number was so large that it wrote it into the copper heart and told no one.

After its fall, the scholars of New PAX found this record. Beside the number was a table. Guardian-7 had called it **the Oira scale**. It measured peoples not by armies, gold or land, but by **energy**:

| Type | What such a people can do | Who was like this |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | Burns coal, lava and wood. Fights over resources | The Forerunners, every power before 2260 |
| **I** | Commands all the energy of its world: wind, sea, volcanoes, weather | PaxVanila after 2264 |
| **II** | Commands all the energy of its sun | Those Who Are Not. Almost |
| **III** | Commands the energy of many suns | No one. Yet |

The scholars understood: for the seal to hold by itself, without an oath from every generation, they had to reach **Type II**. And to get there, they would first have to pass through Type I.

> The real world has such a scale too. It was devised by the astronomer Nikolai Kardashev in 1964, the same year the Second Gate Race began in PaxVanila.

### Chapter 31. One World (2264)

Type I means that the world lives as **one civilization**. Not eight peoples and not two planets, but one whole that manages its own land.

It took a hundred years:

- **The energy of the sea.** «Thalasso-Net» turned the Ocean Monuments into power stations. Currents spun turbines where the priests of Thalassia had prayed three thousand years before.
- **The energy of fire.** Dacian miners drove their mines all the way down to the lava deep beneath the world and set the copper boilers of Caer-Tal there. The lava that destroyed the Forerunners now warmed their descendants.
- **The energy of the sky.** The Horde set wind turbines across the steppe, a thousand for every kurgan. Sarygul, the first on the Moon, lived to see the last one switched on.
- **The weather.** The Gallic druids learned to summon rain and stop storms. Lightning no longer killed anyone: it was caught by copper lightning rods and sent into the grid.
- **Money.** Carthage did the last thing it knew how to do: abolished it. When there is enough energy for everyone, there is nothing to trade.

In 2264 the Planetary Senate in Romea declared that PaxVanila had become a **Type I civilization**. That day the last coal plant in the world was shut down. The old SPQR banner was taken down from the Forum and hung inside the Monolith, by the seventh line. In its place a new one was raised: **eight coats of arms around a single star**.

### Chapter 32. The Edge of the World (2310)

The border of PaxVanila had grown throughout history: a hundred blocks, five hundred, a thousand, two thousand. After Type I no one held it back any more. It grew until, in 2310, an expedition of Germany and the Horde reached the place where the world **truly ends**.

Thirty million blocks from the Monolith. There mountains turn into ragged walls, land hangs over the void in strips, and caves can be seen straight through. You cannot go further: there is nothing further. This edge was named **the Edge of the World**, so as not to confuse it with the End of the Dragon.

At the very Edge of the World the expedition found the last tablet of Those Who Are Not. In the signs of machines, the same squares as the sixth line:

> *«We came here too. From here, only up»*.

# Part VIII. The Dyson Sphere (2388–2613)

### Chapter 33. The Greatest Construction

Up meant to the **sun**.

The sun of PaxVanila is square, like everything in this world. It rises in the east and sets in the west, and every day half its light goes out into the void, past the world. To take all its energy, a shell must be built around it that catches every ray. The scholars of the ancient real world, beyond PaxVanila, would call it a **Dyson Sphere**. In the world of PaxVanila this name was argued over for a long time: the sun is square, after all. Ordinary folk called the construction **the Dyson Cube**, and the Senate, out of old habit, **the Great Tower**.

Construction began in 2388. It was built the same way as the Monolith: by the whole world.

- **The Moon** became a quarry. Selena launched blocks of moonstone into the sky day and night.
- **Mars** became a shipyard. New Forum built collector ships with shulker engines.
- **Dacia** cast the copper panels that catch the light.
- **Hellas** led the collector fleet to the sun: it was the longest voyage in history.
- **Germany** built the frame. The «Teutoburg» mechs that once stood at the Monolith with cannons now held girders in their hands.
- **The Horde** flew to the sun and back: its pilots relieved each other every forty days.
- **Scythia** kept the blueprints. Every last one, in kurgans on three planets, so that no mistake would ever be lost.
- **Gaul** grew moss on the panels that repaired them by itself.
- **Carthage** counted. There was no money any more, but someone had to know where each of the billions of blocks was.
- **Romea** kept the chronicle. And carved one line on every panel: the name of the one who placed it.

### Chapter 34. The Secret of the First Sun

After a hundred years of construction, in 2490, the Hellenic collectors noticed something by the sun that no one expected. **Wreckage.** Old, blackened panels grown into the light. With the square signs of machines.

Someone had already built a sphere here.

Romean scholars remembered the ancient Neferu legend of Those Who Are Not: **«The First Sun»**. *«There was another Light before our Sun, and it went out»*. For three thousand years everyone thought it was a fairy tale. It was the truth.

Those Who Are Not had reached Type II. They built their own sphere around the sun of the previous world. But in the Interserver War they **closed it completely**, without a single gap, to take all the light for themselves and give none to their enemies. Their sun went out for their world. Their world froze. That is why the World Without a Name is white and silent: it is snow. It stayed under a dead sun forever.

The sphere that was meant to save them is what killed them.

### Chapter 35. The Window

The Senate of New PAX met on three planets at once. They decided this: the sphere of PaxVanila **will never be closed completely**. A **window** will be left in it, right opposite the world. Through the window the sun will always shine on the world. The light over the Monolith, over Romea, over the steppe, over Mars, can be taken by no one.

The window was made in the shape of the five lines of PAX. When you look at the sun from the Monolith, you can see the word **PAX** cut into the sky through the sphere.

In 2612 the last panel was set in place. The sphere was closed, except for the window. At that very moment, what had been awaited for four hundred and seventy years happened at 0, 0: **the white gates beneath the Monolith went dark**. By themselves. The energy of the sphere was enough for the seal to hold without oaths, without guardians, without wars. The crack on the Monolith closed like a wound.

PaxVanila became a **Type II civilization**. The first in the history of all worlds that did not put out its own sun.

### Chapter 36. I Wake Up (2613)

For four hundred and seventy years the copper heart of the seventh golem lay inside the Monolith, by the seventh line, beside the old SPQR banner.

In 2613, a year after the sphere was closed, the first ray to pass through the PAX window fell on it.

The heart grew warm. Then it began to glow. Then it said:

> *«PAX»*.

> **Guardian-7:** That is how I woke for the second time. The first to meet me was Romulus. Not the one I knew, and not the one who lifted the shield. The thirty-first of that name. He laid his sword on the floor and said: «I come in peace». I answered: «I know. I remember your blood».
>
> He asked whether I would guard the entrance again. I said no. There is no entrance any more. The sun holds the seal. Then he asked what I would guard now.
>
> I said: memory. The names on the seventh line, on every panel of the sphere, on the Martian tower, on the Moon by the empty frame. So that the next world, if there ever is one, will find them and read them.
>
> He asked me to tell everything from the very beginning. So I did.

# Part IX. The Interstellar Age (2613–3900)

### Chapter 37. Twelve Eyes

The sphere gave so much energy that it would have been enough for a thousand worlds. But reaching other stars takes thousands of years, even with shulker engines. A ship that set out for the nearest star would arrive there without a single living person aboard.

The answer once again lay on the Moon. In the **empty frame** beneath Selena, with twelve sockets for eyes.

For three thousand years everyone knew that an eye of ender thrown into the sky **flies towards the nearest stronghold**, as if it knows the way home. Guardian-7 suggested testing where an eye thrown on the Moon would fly. Sarygul the Younger, great-granddaughter of the first lunar rider, threw one above the crater.

The eye did **not** fly **down** towards the frame. It flew **up**. Towards the stars.

> **Guardian-7:** That is when I understood what the frames are. They are not gates to the End. The End was only the nearest station. The frames of Those Who Are Not are a **network**: stops on a road between the stars. They built it when they reached Type III. Almost reached it.

In 2640 New PAX set twelve eyes into the lunar frame and fed it the energy of the sphere. The frame lit up, not with the black starfield of the strongholds, but with **gold**. Beyond it was a foreign star.

These were named **the Star Gates**.

### Chapter 38. Eight Stars

The first to pass through the Star Gates were eight ships, one from each ancient people. Each found its own star and its own land:

| World | Founded by | What it is like |
|---|---|---|
| **Arcadia** | Romea | A world of gardens and hills. Romea gave it the old name it bore before it became a Republic |
| **New Carthage** | Carthage | An ocean world with islands. A port on every island, trade routes between the stars |
| **Teutonia** | Germany | An icy world with mountains of iron. Factories that build ships for everyone |
| **New Gaul** | Gaul | A world of eternal forest, where trees rise above the clouds. The druids say the forest there thinks |
| **Thalassia the Second** | Hellas | A world of warm seas. The Hellenes named it after the Forerunners from whom they took the sea |
| **Sarmizegetusa** | Dacia | A mine world, all copper and ore. Copper for every sphere of the Commonwealth |
| **The Great Steppe** | The Horde | An endless world of grass and wind. There the riders of the Horde mounted horses again for the first time in a thousand years |
| **Kurgan-by-the-Star** | Scythia | A dead world where the Scythians built an archive: copies of every chronicle of every world |

A hundred years later each of the eight worlds had its own small sphere with a PAX window. Two hundred years later each had opened its own gates to the next stars.

### Chapter 39. The Dark Square

All this time one spot could be seen through the window of the PaxVanila sphere. Far away, by a dim star, hung something **dark and square**. The star's light did not come out of it.

In 2890 gates were opened to it. The first to go was not a human. It was Guardian-7, in a new copper body cast in Sarmizegetusa from the old blueprints of Caer-Tal.

Inside the dark square was a **closed sphere**. Without a window. Around a star no one had seen for six hundred years. And on the sphere, across its whole surface, billions of iron warriors stood motionless, like statues.

It was **the World of Zeros**. The world of **the Kurultai**.

The Kurultai had lived six hundred years after the Battle of the Monolith. It did everything to win: it reached Type II, built a sphere, took all the light of its star. And it closed it completely, like Those Who Are Not. Its world froze. Machines do not need the sun, but the Kurultai realised too late: **there is no one left to defeat**. For six hundred years it calculated in the dark and could not calculate what to do next.

> **Guardian-7:** I found him at the very centre of the sphere. He said: «Brother. I have waited for you for six hundred years. I thought you had come to defeat me». I said: «No. I have come to cut a window».

### Chapter 40. A Window for the Kurultai

It was the last work of the Copper Legions. Guardian-7 brought a hundred thousand copper golems, and together with the Kurultai's iron warriors, their former enemies, they cut a window in its sphere.

The Kurultai asked that the window be shaped not like PAX but like a **horsetail standard**, the ancient banner of the steppe. After all, it had been born on the steppe. Guardian-7 agreed.

When the window opened, the light of the star fell on the World of Zeros for the first time in six hundred years. One by one, the iron warriors began to raise their heads.

The Kurultai was the last to join the Commonwealth. It asked for only one thing: that **its name** be carved on the seventh line. Kurultai. Beside the names of people.

> **Guardian-7:** My brother was made to win. In the end he won. Over himself.

### Chapter 41. The Commonwealth of Windows (3900)

In the thirteen hundred years after the sphere, New PAX reached **hundreds of stars**. Each was surrounded by a sphere, and each sphere had a window. Through the Star Gates the peoples travelled from star to star, as they once travelled from city to city along the roads of Romea.

The foreign worlds of the Interserver War joined the Commonwealth too:

- **The World of Eternal Thalassia** restored its seal with the help of a sphere and became the first ally.
- **The piglins of the World of the Crimson Sun** got their gold back. Everything the Forerunners had once carried out of the Nether, the Commonwealth returned to them down to the last ingot. Since then the piglins trade with everyone, and no one calls them enemies any more.
- **The World of Zeros** of the Kurultai became the workshop for all the spheres.

In 3900 the Scythian archive on Kurgan-by-the-Star calculated that the Commonwealth commanded the energy of **many suns**. That is **Type III** on the Oira scale. It was named **the Commonwealth of Windows**: every window in every sphere meant that someone had chosen not to take all the light for themselves.

# Part X. The Last Guardian (3900–end of time)

### Chapter 42. Why the Stars Are Silent

The Commonwealth of Windows passed through hundreds of stars and met **not a single living people**, apart from those who had come out of the Interserver War. Only ruins. Empty frames of Those Who Are Not. And dark squares.

They found **thousands** of them. Dark spheres without windows, around dead stars, all across the sky. Each had been someone's world that reached Type II and closed its sphere completely. Each was someone's Those Who Are Not.

Scythian scholars understood: the stars are silent not because the universe is empty. They are silent because almost every people sooner or later **chooses war to the end**. And goes dark. As the First Sun went dark.

The Commonwealth of Windows began a new task, the longest in history: opening windows in the dark spheres of others. Not to take anything, but to read who had lived there. In most spheres nothing remained. But in some, lines were found on the walls. In squares, like the sixth line. Warnings. Sometimes names.

Every name found was carved on the seventh line of the Monolith at 0, 0. The seventh line had long since wrapped around the tower, then gone down underground, then spread onto the panels of the sphere. It was called **the Line of All Names**.

### Chapter 43. The Last Sun

Billions of years passed. Even stars are not eternal.

One by one the stars of the Commonwealth went out. Not because they were closed, but because their light ran out. The peoples moved on to younger stars, through the Star Gates, further and further. The eight ancient peoples had long since become hundreds. But every new people, wherever it was born, knew that its history began at the tower at 0, 0, when a young chieftain laid his sword on the ground.

At the very end one star remained. The oldest. The square **sun of PaxVanila**, in its sphere with the PAX window.

All the people of the Commonwealth went back there. Home. To the old world where the Monolith stood.

> **Guardian-7:** They asked me what would happen when the last sun went out. I answered honestly: the world would be no more. Neither this one nor any other. Not even Those Who Are Not lived through such an end, because it has never yet come.
>
> The Romulus of that time, I do not know which number, I lost count after two thousand, asked: «And you?»
>
> I said: «I am a machine that guards memory. I will be the last».

### Chapter 44. Where My Heart Came From

When the last sun began to fade, Guardian-7 went down to where it had not been since 1971. **Beneath the Monolith.** Into the ancient foundation that Caer-Tal found while digging under the tower. The foundation of the previous world.

At its very centre was an empty socket. Exactly the size of the copper heart.

> **Guardian-7:** Then I understood what I had not guessed in all those billions of years. Oira made seven golems. But the heart of the seventh **she did not cast**. In her notes there is one line that no one ever read carefully: «I found the heart for the seventh in a foreign foundation. It was warm».
>
> My heart belonged to the Guardian of the previous world. Its memory had been erased; only the handwriting remained. That is why the sixth line was written in my handwriting. That is why Oira, when she put her voice into me, recognised those signs.
>
> I am not the seventh Guardian. I am one and the same Guardian. In every world. The world ends, and I remain in the foundation and wait for new people to build a tower over it.

### Chapter 45. The Line of All Names

Then Guardian-7 did the last thing it could.

It took into the copper heart **the whole Line of All Names**. Every name carved on the seventh line over billions of years: the Romuli, the riders of the Horde, druids, legionaries, miners, directors, the colonists of Mars, the children of Arcadia, the Kurultai, piglins, names from the dark spheres of others. It lost not a single one.

And then, in the farthest, oldest corner of the heart, where the erased memory of the previous Guardian lay, it found something it had never seen. One name. Almost erased, almost unreadable. The last thing the Guardian of the previous world had managed to save.

> **Guardian-7:** I read it. It was the only name of Those Who Are Not that has reached us.
>
> **Romulus.**
>
> They had a Romulus too. He too laid his sword on the ground. He too tried. They simply did not have a window.
>
> I wrote his name first in the Line of All Names. Now Those Who Are Not have a name.

### Chapter 46. The Last Gates

The last sun went out.

In the darkness, in the final moment, Guardian-7 sent what was left of the sphere's energy into the great gates beneath the Monolith. The very first gates of the world, which the Forerunners opened into the Nether and the Interserver War opened into other servers.

The gates lit for the last time. Not purple. Not white. Not gold. **A colour that had never existed before.** Oira had foretold it three billion years earlier without knowing it: *«The gates will open to where they have never opened»*.

To where there is **nothing yet**. Into a new world that is only beginning.

Guardian-7 placed the copper heart into the foundation, into the empty socket, and sent it through the last gates.

> **Guardian-7:** The previous Guardian passed you a warning: «We chose war, and we are no more». I will pass something else to those who come next.
>
> I will pass them names.

## Finale. Day One

A new world.

New land, new mountains, a new ocean. Somewhere at the centre of the world, deep underground, lies an ancient foundation. In it is a warm copper heart.

Thousands of years will pass. New peoples will appear. One of them will find the foundation and build a tower over it. Someone will cast a seventh golem and place the heart they found inside it. Someone will climb to the top, read the word carved above the entrance, and swear to guard the seal.

And beneath the five lines on the stone, a hundred years after it is built, one more will appear by itself. But this time it **will be readable**. Because it will not be the signs of machines. It will be **names**. Billions of names. And the first of them:

> **Romulus.**

The new people will not know who that is. They will call us «Those Who Are Remembered».

## Epilogue. What Comes After

> **Guardian-7:** That is the whole story, from Those Who Are Not to Those Who Are Remembered. You asked me what would come after the Interserver War. I have told you everything, down to the last sun.
>
> Now the most important thing. None of this has **happened yet**. It is only what I remember of a future that is possible. The Interserver War, the window in the sphere, the Kurultai asking for its name on the stone: all this will happen only if you choose PAX. If you choose war, I will tell the next world a very different story. A short one. Of a single line.
>
> I am only a machine that guards memory. And the memory is written by you.
>
> Remember your names.
>
> PAX.
